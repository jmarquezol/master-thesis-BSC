\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

We now test the \ac{CFT} predictions of Section~\ref{sec:cft:temporal} against simulations of the critical ANNNI-type chain (Eq.~\eqref{eq:annni_H} with $\lambda=1$), quenched from the polarized state $\ket{X+}$. We vary the \ac{NNN} coupling from the integrable point, $p=0$, up to $p=0.5$. Because extracting the universal data reliably requires a sufficiently wide time window, and the simulation cost grows significantly with $p$, we restrict the dynamical calculations to $p\le0.5$ for simplicity and because it suffices for extracting the universal properties from the critical system.

The analysis divides into two complementary routes. The entropy route uses the transfer-matrix eigenvectors to compute the generalized R\'enyi-2 entropy (Eq.~\eqref{eq:s2gen}) and compares it with the conformal profile (Eq.~\eqref{eq:cft:chord}). The spectral route uses the eigenvalues alone to verify emergent dual unitarity, extract the central charge from the phase curvature of the leading eigenvalue (Eq.~\eqref{eq:cft:eq3}), and determine the boundary exponent from the first spectral gap (Eq.~\eqref{eq:cft:eq4}). In both routes, we evaluate the integrable Ising point ($p=0$) alongside the non-integrable cases ($p>0$) to test whether the conformal predictions remain valid in the presence of the \ac{NNN} interaction. Table~\ref{tab:cp} collects the universal data obtained from both routes, and the rest of this section explains how each value is measured.

Unless stated otherwise, all simulations use the second-order \textit{VD2} \ac{MPO} of Section~\ref{sec:mporep} with time step $\delta t=0.1$, cooling regulator $\beta_0=0.2$ ($N_\beta=4$), and maximum temporal bond dimension $\chi=64$. We convert all spectral quantities into universal data using the independently determined sound velocities $v_\infty(p)$ from Section~\ref{sec:annni:velocity}, with fitting conventions and controls detailed in Appendix~\ref{app:errors}.

\subsection{The entropy route}
\label{sec:results:validation}
\label{sec:results:wall}

At the integrable point, the generalized temporal entropy follows the conformal prediction established for the Loschmidt echo of the critical Ising chain~\cite{carignano_tagliacozzo2025}. We find that this behaviour persists when the \ac{NNN} interaction is introduced. Figure~\ref{fig:domes} shows the R\'enyi-2 profiles at $p=0.1$. The real part follows the chord dependence in Eq.~\eqref{eq:cft:chord}, while the imaginary part is approximately constant in the interior of the strip. For this comparison, we fix $c=\tfrac12$ and adjust only the non-universal constant $s_0$, following the analysis of the integrable chain. Appendix~\ref{app:highp} presents the corresponding profiles at the remaining couplings.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{imgs/res_domes.png}
    \caption{\textbf{The generalized temporal entropy at $p=0.1$.} Each colour denotes one evolution time. (a) Real part as a function of $t/T$. The dashed curves show the conformal profile in Eq.~\eqref{eq:cft:chord} with $c=\tfrac12$. (b) Imaginary part, which is approximately constant in the interior of the strip. Its plateau lies above the asymptotic value $\pi c/16$ (dashed line) because of finite-time corrections that decrease with increasing $T$.}
    \label{fig:domes}
\end{figure}

At every simulated time, the measured plateau lies above the asymptotic value $\pi c/16$. This offset is the finite-time correction discussed in Section~\ref{sec:cft:temporal}~\cite{boucomas2026}. Because it decays slowly with $T$, the asymptotic plateau must be obtained by extrapolating a sequence of evolution times. A short time series cannot distinguish the decay from a constant offset and therefore does not constrain the asymptote. Including the finite-time correction gives $c=\OPEN$ at the integrable point, consistent with the exact value $\tfrac12$, and $c=\OPEN$ at $p=0.1$. In contrast to Figure~\ref{fig:domes}, where $c$ is fixed to test the predicted profile, these extrapolations determine $c$ from the data. The results are included in Table~\ref{tab:cp}.

We use the imaginary plateau rather than the real-part chord slope to estimate the central charge. At each evolution time, Eq.~\eqref{eq:cft:chord} predicts the plateau value directly. By contrast, extracting $c$ from the real part requires a two-parameter fit of the chord coefficient and the non-universal offset $s_0$, making the result more sensitive to the interval of the strip included in the fit.

The accessible time window decreases as $p$ increases, leaving fewer points with which to determine the finite-time decay. The latest accessible times also require the repetition procedure described in Section~\ref{sec:reach}. When the available window is too short to constrain the extrapolation, we do not report an entropy-based central charge and instead use the spectral method below.


\subsection{The spectral route}
\label{sec:results:spectral}


\begin{wrapfigure}{r}{0.46\textwidth}
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{imgs/res_circle.png}
    \caption{\textbf{Trajectory of the normalised leading eigenvalue.} At each coupling, $\mu_0$ is divided by its mean modulus over the time series. The phase winds with $T$, while the weak variation of the modulus keeps the points close to the dashed unit circle.}
    \label{fig:circle}
\end{wrapfigure}

Using the eigenvalue-only method of Section~\ref{sec:blockpm}, we compute the spectrum up to $T=24$ at the integrable point and up to $T=20$ at the remaining couplings. Only the shorter fitting windows listed in Table~\ref{tab:cp} are used to extract universal quantities. Figure~\ref{fig:circle} shows the trajectory of $\mu_0$ in the complex plane after normalising each time series by its mean modulus. The phase winds throughout the simulated interval, while the modulus varies by less than one per cent. This behaviour is consistent with the emergent dual unitarity described in Section~\ref{sec:cft:temporal}. The corresponding central-charge fits are shown in Figure~\ref{fig:eq3}.

We extract the central charge from the phase curvature using Eq.~\eqref{eq:cft:eq3}. Each time series is sampled with $\Delta T=0.5$ because the fit is constrained by both the extent of the window and the number of points within it. The fitting conventions are given in Appendix~\ref{app:errors}.

Table~\ref{tab:cp} summarises the results. The spectral estimates remain within approximately ten per cent of the Ising value at every coupling and are consistent with $c=\tfrac12$ within the resolution of the fits. The equilibrium chord analysis of Section~\ref{sec:annni:equilibrium} gives $c\approx0.502$ over the same range. These determinations use independent data: the equilibrium estimate is obtained from the ground state, whereas the spectral estimate follows from the real-time dynamics.

The sound velocity affects the calculation in two competing ways. Finite-time corrections are controlled by $vT$, so their relative magnitude decreases as $v$ grows at fixed $T$. However, the phase gaps also scale as $1/(vT)$ and therefore become more difficult to resolve. In addition, converting the fitted curvature into a central charge multiplies its numerical uncertainty by $v$. These resolution requirements cause the fitting windows in Table~\ref{tab:cp} to shorten with increasing $p$, even though finite-time corrections within those windows become smaller.

The Ising boundary tower persists when the \ac{NNN} interaction is introduced. As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:tower} and Table~\ref{tab:cp}, the leading dimension remains close to $\tfrac12$ at every coupling. At fixed evolution time, its deviation from this value decreases as $p$ increases, consistent with finite-time corrections controlled by $vT$.

Resolving the higher levels requires a larger block. With $k=8$, the first three dimensions approach the Ising values $\tfrac12$, $\tfrac32$, and $2$ at every non-integrable coupling shown in Figure~\ref{fig:tower}. The remaining Ritz values do not converge sufficiently within the accessible time windows, so we do not assign them to higher members of the conformal tower.
