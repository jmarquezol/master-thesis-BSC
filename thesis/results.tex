\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
\label{sec:results:limits}

The four predictions of Section~\ref{sec:cft:temporal} are now confronted with the simulations. They split into two families, and this section follows that split. The entropy route goes through the eigenvectors: the generalized R\'enyi-2 entropy of Eq.~\eqref{eq:s2gen} is compared with the conformal profile of Eq.~\eqref{eq:cft:chord}. The spectral route goes through the eigenvalues alone: emergent dual unitarity, the central charge from the phase curvature of $\lambda_0$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:cft:eq3}), and the boundary exponent from the first gap (Eq.~\eqref{eq:cft:eq4}). In each route the integrable point is measured first---where every prediction has an exact target~\cite{carignano_tagliacozzo2025}---and frustration is switched on afterwards. The last subsection presents a structure of the interacting spectrum that the boundary-exponent measurement uncovered.

Unless stated otherwise, all runs use the VD2 time-evolution MPO (Section~\ref{sec:mporep}) with $\delta t=0.1$, the regulator $\beta_0=0.2$ ($N_\beta=4$, Section~\ref{sec:cft:boundary}), the initial state $\ket{X+}^{\otimes N}$, and the block power method of Section~\ref{sec:numerics} with $k=4$ states at bond dimension $\chi=64$, escalated to larger blocks where the spectrum demands it; the deep spectra of Section~\ref{sec:results:tower} use $k=8$. Eigenvalue-based measurements use the measured sound velocity $v(p)$ (Appendix~\ref{app:velocity}).

\subsection{The entropy route}
\label{sec:results:entropy}
\label{sec:results:validation}
\label{sec:results:wall}

At $p=0$ the temporal entropy follows the conformal prediction over the full computed window. Figure~\ref{fig:domes_p0} shows the R\'enyi-2 profiles: the real part collapses onto the chord curves of Eq.~\eqref{eq:cft:chord} with $c=\tfrac12$, and the imaginary part forms the predicted plateau. The plateau sits above $\pi c/16$: at accessible times it carries a finite-time correction $\propto T^{-1/2}$~\cite{boucomas2026}. Fitting that form and extrapolating turns the plateau into an absolute estimate of the central charge, which descends from $0.61$ to $0.53$ as the fit window grows and continues towards $\tfrac12$.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{imgs/res_domes_p0.png}
    \caption{\textbf{The temporal entropy at the integrable point.} (a) Re $S_2$ against $t/T$ with the $c=\tfrac12$ chord curves of Eq.~\eqref{eq:cft:chord} (dashed). (b) Im $S_2$ with the $\pi c/16$ plateau (dashed); the offset is the finite-time correction. (c) The absolute central charge from the corrected plateau fit, descending towards $\tfrac12$ as the fit window grows.}
    \label{fig:domes_p0}
\end{figure}

Switching on frustration, the dome remains conformal inside a window that shrinks with $p$. Extracting the R\'enyi-2 slope over $T=4$--$9$ at $p=0.1$, and calibrating against $p=0$ so that the non-universal finite-$T$, finite-$\chi$ and finite-$\delta t$ prefactors cancel in the ratio (Eq.~\eqref{eq:cft:calibratio}), gives
\begin{equation}
    c(p=0.1) = 0.47 \pm 0.05,
    \label{eq:results:headline}
\end{equation}
with per-time values $0.43,\,0.46,\,0.56,\,0.48,\,0.46,\,0.42$, consistent with the Ising value. The quoted uncertainty is the sample standard deviation of these per-time reads. At $p=0.3$ and $p=0.5$ the clean window closes almost immediately---at $T\approx3$ and $T\approx2$---so too few rungs remain below the wall for a chord fit, and no central charge can be extracted from the entropy there. The entropy route under frustration is therefore quantitative at $p=0.1$ and only qualitative beyond.

The window is closed by the wall anticipated in Section~\ref{sec:reach}. Figure~\ref{fig:wall}a shows the phase rigidity of the dominant pair, Eq.~\eqref{eq:rigidity}, collapsing geometrically at every coupling, and ever faster as $p$ grows. It reaches its floor exactly where the profiles leave the conformal line, at $T=10$ for $p=0.1$ (Figure~\ref{fig:wall}b). The leading spectrum, by contrast, stays smooth there and consistent between independent runs, so what fails is the object being asked for rather than the solver. Appendix~\ref{app:wall} collects the supporting checks: no post-processing of the converged subspace rescues the entropy, and the wall passes all three resolution tests of Section~\ref{sec:reach} unchanged. Those same runs settle the comparison promised in Section~\ref{sec:transverse:truncation}: where both schemes converge they agree on every eigenvalue and on the rigidity to four to five digits (Appendix~\ref{app:wall:eps}). Past the wall the physics must be read from the eigenvalues.

    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{imgs/res_wall.png}
    \caption{\textbf{Where the eigenvector route ends.} (a) Phase rigidity of the dominant pair against $T$: a geometric collapse at every $p$, steeper as frustration grows. (b) The $p=0.1$ profiles against the chord variable: the $T\le9$ points (squares) sit on the clean-window fit, the $T\ge10$ points (crosses) leave it.}
    \label{fig:wall}
\end{figure}

\subsection{The spectral route}
\label{sec:results:spectral}

The eigenvalue-only mode of Section~\ref{sec:blockpm} carries the spectral measurements far past the wall: to $T=20$ at $p=0$. Figure~\ref{fig:spectral_p0}a traces $\lambda_0$ in the complex plane over that whole ladder: the modulus is constant at $1.487$ while the phase winds---emergent dual unitarity, prediction 4. Figure~\ref{fig:spectral_p0}b shows the central charge from the same eigenvalue: the corrected fit of Eq.~\eqref{eq:cft:eq3} returns $c=0.48$. The long window also settles the branch-constant question of Appendix~\ref{app:eq3}. Written for the unnormalized phase, the fit carries a constant term $B$ alongside the Casimir coefficient, and on short windows the two trade off against each other. Left free over $T\le20$ the fit finds $B=-3.141$ on its own, so at the integrable point the two are no longer degenerate and the absolute read stands without pinning.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{imgs/res_spectral_p0.png}
    \caption{\textbf{The spectral predictions at the integrable point, $T=2$--$20$.} (a) $\lambda_0$ in the complex plane: constant modulus, winding phase. (b) The corrected Eq.~\eqref{eq:cft:eq3} fit of the de-biased phase, giving $c=0.48$.}
    \label{fig:spectral_p0}
\end{figure}

The boundary exponent distinguishes the two boundary conditions of Section~\ref{sec:cft:temporal}. Fitting Eq.~\eqref{eq:cft:eq4} to the first spectral gap gives $x_1=0.49$ for the free boundary ($\ket{X+}$) and $x_1=2.07$ for the fixed one ($\ket{Up}$), against the exact values $\tfrac12$ and $2$ (Figure~\ref{fig:x1_p0}).

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.65\textwidth]{imgs/res_x1_p0.png}
    \caption{\textbf{The boundary exponent at the integrable point.} The first spectral gap against $T$ for the free and fixed boundary states with the fits of Eq.~\eqref{eq:cft:eq4}, giving $x_1=0.49$ and $2.07$ against the exact $\tfrac12$ and $2$.}
    \label{fig:x1_p0}
\end{figure}

Under frustration the spectral route survives where the entropy route has already failed. Figure~\ref{fig:partner_gap}b shows $|\lambda_0|(T)$ for every coupling: each arm sits on its own constant radius, so dual unitarity persists across the frustrated range. Figure~\ref{fig:partner_gap}a shows why the measurements nevertheless become harder: the modulus of the nearest $\pi$-displaced partner closes on $|\lambda_0|$ to within one per cent from $T\approx4$ at every $p$, which is the near-degeneracy that forces the phase-predicted branch tracking of Section~\ref{sec:numerics:practical}. At $p=0.1$ that tracking follows the physical branch to $T=11$, and the partner indeed overtakes the physical branch in modulus from $T\approx6$. At $p\ge0.3$ the phase advance per rung outruns the $T$-spacing of the ladder and the branch is lost after a few rungs, so the eigenvalue central charge is not yet extractable there.

    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{imgs/res_partner_gap.png}
    \caption{\textbf{The spectrum under frustration.} (a) Modulus ratio between $\lambda_0$ and its nearest $\pi$-displaced partner: within one per cent of degeneracy from $T\approx4$ at every $p$. (b) $|\lambda_0|(T)$ per coupling: each arm holds its own constant radius, emergent dual unitarity across the frustrated range.}
    \label{fig:partner_gap}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:eq3_p} shows the same fit at $p=0.1$. The branch constant again settles on $-\pi$ without being asked to, and over the same $T\le9$ window the measured velocity $v(0.1)=2.60$ gives $c=0.54$, which two independent ladders reproduce ($0.536$ from the eigenvalue-only arm, $0.572$ from the full-eigenvector one). Both are consistent with the Ising value at the accuracy the integrable point itself achieves, where the same construction returns $0.48$.

The window matters, and $B$ is what reveals it. Extending the $p=0.1$ fit to $T\le11$ halves the Casimir coefficient and drops the read to $0.32$, and at the same time $B$ drifts from $-3.144$ to $-3.148$; pushing further makes $C$ change sign altogether. At $p=0$ nothing of the sort happens, and $B$ holds at $-3.141$ over the whole ladder to $T=20$. The departure of $B$ from $-\pi$ therefore acts as an internal check on the fit, and it fails at $p=0.1$ exactly where the branch tracking of Section~\ref{sec:numerics:practical} begins to lose the physical eigenvalue among its near-degenerate partners. Table~\ref{tab:cp} collects the measured universal data.

\subsection{The momentum-\texorpdfstring{$\pi$}{pi} displaced boundary tower}
\label{sec:results:tower}

Read naively, the boundary-exponent measurement fails at $p\neq0$. Taking the subleading eigenvalue nearest $\lambda_0$ in phase, as at the integrable point, Eq.~\eqref{eq:cft:eq4} returns $x_1\approx\tfrac32$ at $p=0.1$---a heavier operator than the free-boundary $\tfrac12$, as if the lightest state had disappeared from the spectrum.

The deep $k=8$ spectra show where it went. Figure~\ref{fig:tower} places the block members in the plane of phase gap to $\lambda_0$ versus relative modulus: one family sits at small phase gaps, at the positions of the even-parity identity ladder $x=2,3,4$, and a second family enters from phase $\pi$, at the positions of the odd-parity ladder $x=\tfrac12,\tfrac32,\tfrac52$ read from the $\pi$ side. Nothing is missing---the entire odd ladder, which contains the lightest boundary operator, is displaced in phase by momentum $\pi$. Reading each eigenvalue on its own side restores $x_1=0.49$ at $p=0$ and $x_1=0.47$ at $p=0.1$, the free-boundary value at both couplings.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{imgs/res_tower_spectrum.png}
    \caption{\textbf{The two boundary ladders in the deep spectrum.} The $k=8$ block at $p=0.1$ in the plane of phase gap to $\lambda_0$ versus relative modulus, for three evolution times. Blue: even (identity) ladder at the guides $x=2,3,4$; red: odd (boundary-fermion) ladder at the guides $x=\tfrac12,\tfrac32,\tfrac52$ entering from the $\pi$ side.}
    \label{fig:tower}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:tower_p} shows the same reading along the eigenvalue ladders. At $p=0$ the full free-boundary ladder $\{\tfrac12,\tfrac32,2\}$ sits near $\lambda_0$ and holds flat from $T=2$ to $20$; there is no displaced family, as expected for free fermions. At $p=0.1$ the ladder splits: the identity operator $x=2$ stays near $\lambda_0$ while the odd operators $\{\tfrac12,\tfrac32\}$ appear on the $\pi$ side, flat in $T$ up to $T\approx11$. Turning on the interaction is what moves the odd ladder, and the displacement is the dynamical consequence of the parity-dressed hopping of Section~\ref{sec:annni:jw}: the \ac{NNN} term attaches a momentum $\pi$ to the odd-fermion-parity sector (Appendix~\ref{app:pairing}, including why a free-fermion chain cannot produce the displacement). At $p\ge0.3$ the same reading is not yet conclusive: the subleading block members are the least converged ones, and the compressed phase ladder mixes the families within the short trackable window, so those panels are left to the finer cluster ladders.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{imgs/res_tower_across_p.png}
    \caption{\textbf{The displaced tower appears with the interaction.} Boundary dimensions read from the eigenvalue ladders with the measured $v(p)$, blue for states near $\lambda_0$ and red for states read from the $\pi$ side; dashed lines mark $x=\tfrac12,\tfrac32,2,3$. At $p=0$ the whole ladder sits near $\lambda_0$; at $p=0.1$ the odd operators have moved to the $\pi$ side and stay flat in $T$.}
    \label{fig:tower_p}
\end{figure}

The two ladders also explain how the numerics degrade with frustration, for the reason given in Section~\ref{sec:numerics:practical}: the near-degenerate partner that limits the branch tracking is the displaced image of $\lambda_0$ itself, and the larger velocity at larger $p$ compresses both ladders in phase. The universal content, wherever it can be measured, remains that of the Ising boundary \ac{CFT}; what frustration changes is how much of it is within reach.
