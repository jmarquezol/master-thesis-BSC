\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

The four predictions of Section~\ref{sec:cft:temporal} are now confronted with the simulations. They split into two families, and this section follows that split. The entropy route goes through the eigenvectors: the generalized R\'enyi-2 entropy of Eq.~\eqref{eq:s2gen} is compared with the conformal profile of Eq.~\eqref{eq:cft:chord}. The spectral route goes through the eigenvalues alone. It carries three of the predictions: emergent dual unitarity, the central charge from the phase curvature of $\lambda_0$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:cft:eq3}), and the boundary exponent from the first gap (Eq.~\eqref{eq:cft:eq4}). In each route the integrable point is measured first, where every prediction has an exact target~\cite{carignano_tagliacozzo2025}, and frustration is switched on afterwards. The last subsection returns to the boundary exponent, whose measurement uncovered a feature of the interacting spectrum.

Unless stated otherwise, all runs use the VD2 time-evolution MPO of Section~\ref{sec:mporep} with $\delta t=0.1$, the regulator $\beta_0=0.2$ ($N_\beta=4$, Section~\ref{sec:cft:boundary}), and the initial state $\ket{X+}^{\otimes N}$. The temporal states come from the block power method of Section~\ref{sec:numerics}, with $k=4$ at bond dimension $\chi=64$ and larger blocks where the spectrum demands them; the deep spectra of Section~\ref{sec:results:tower} use $k=8$. Every eigenvalue-based measurement uses the measured sound velocity $v(p)$ of Appendix~\ref{app:velocity}.

\subsection{The entropy route}
\label{sec:results:validation}
\label{sec:results:wall}

At $p=0$ the temporal entropy follows the conformal prediction over the full computed window. Figure~\ref{fig:domes_p0} shows the R\'enyi-2 profiles: the real part collapses onto the chord curves of Eq.~\eqref{eq:cft:chord} with $c=\tfrac12$, and the imaginary part forms the predicted plateau. The plateau sits above $\pi c/16$: at accessible times it carries a finite-time correction $\propto T^{-1/2}$~\cite{boucomas2026}. Fitting that form and extrapolating turns the plateau into an absolute estimate of the central charge, which descends from $0.61$ to $0.53$ as the fit window grows and continues towards $\tfrac12$.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{imgs/res_domes_p0.png}
    \caption{\textbf{The temporal entropy at the integrable point.} (a) Re $S_2$ against $t/T$ with the $c=\tfrac12$ chord curves of Eq.~\eqref{eq:cft:chord} (dashed). (b) Im $S_2$ with the $\pi c/16$ plateau (dashed); the offset is the finite-time correction. (c) The absolute central charge from the corrected plateau fit, descending towards $\tfrac12$ as the fit window grows.}
    \label{fig:domes_p0}
\end{figure}

Switching on frustration, the dome remains conformal inside a window that shrinks with $p$. We extract the R\'enyi-2 slope over $T=4$--$9$ at $p=0.1$ and calibrate it against $p=0$, so that the non-universal finite-$T$, finite-$\chi$ and finite-$\delta t$ prefactors cancel in the ratio of Eq.~\eqref{eq:cft:calibratio}. This gives
\begin{equation}
    c(p=0.1) = 0.47 \pm 0.05,
    \label{eq:results:headline}
\end{equation}
with per-time values $0.43,\,0.46,\,0.56,\,0.48,\,0.46,\,0.42$, consistent with the Ising value. The quoted uncertainty is the sample standard deviation of these per-time reads. At $p=0.3$ and $p=0.5$ the window closes almost immediately, at $T\approx3$ and $T\approx2$. Two rungs and one rung are not a conformal window, and the slopes they give are an order of magnitude above $\tfrac12$, so no central charge follows from the entropy at those couplings. They are absent from Figure~\ref{fig:wall} for the same reason: there is nothing to show but the inflation that follows the wall. The entropy route is therefore quantitative at $p=0.1$ and silent beyond it.

The window is closed by the wall anticipated in Section~\ref{sec:reach}. Figure~\ref{fig:wall}a shows the phase rigidity of the dominant pair, Eq.~\eqref{eq:rigidity}, collapsing geometrically at every coupling, and ever faster as $p$ grows. It reaches its floor exactly where the profiles leave the conformal line, at $T=10$ for $p=0.1$ (Figure~\ref{fig:wall}b).

The way they leave it is worth reading. The chord variable is not monotonic in the cut position: it rises to a maximum at mid-strip and falls again, so every profile is traced twice, once on each side. A conformal profile depends on the cut only through $W$, so the two halves fall on top of each other and the dome collapses onto a line. Once the entropy stops being a function of $W$ alone the halves separate and the profile opens into the detached arc of the figure. The arc is thus not scatter but the shape of the broken dome, and it appears in the same step where the peak jumps from $0.34$ to $0.72$ and a second maximum forms (Appendix~\ref{app:wall:shape}).

The leading spectrum stays smooth across that step and agrees between independent runs, so what fails is the object being asked for rather than the solver. Appendix~\ref{app:wall} collects the supporting checks: no post-processing of the converged subspace rescues the entropy, and the wall passes all three resolution tests of Section~\ref{sec:reach} unchanged. Widening the regulator does not move it either. The rigidity collapses at the same rate for every $\beta_0$ between $0.1$ and $0.8$, so the linear growth of the modulus gaps with $\beta_0$ does not carry over to the conditioning of the eigenvectors, which is what decides where the route ends. Those runs also settle the comparison promised in Section~\ref{sec:transverse:truncation}, since the two truncation schemes agree on every eigenvalue and on the rigidity to four or five digits wherever both converge (Appendix~\ref{app:wall:eps}). Past the wall the physics must be read from the eigenvalues.

    \caption{\textbf{Where the eigenvector route ends.} (a) Phase rigidity of the dominant pair against $T$: a geometric collapse at every $p$, steeper as frustration grows. (b) Profiles against the chord variable, which rises to a maximum at mid-strip and falls again, so each profile is traced twice. \end{figure}

\subsection{The spectral route}
\label{sec:results:spectral}

The eigenvalue-only mode of Section~\ref{sec:blockpm} carries the spectral measurements far past the wall: to $T=20$ at $p=0$. Figure~\ref{fig:spectral_p0}a traces $\lambda_0$ in the complex plane over that whole ladder: the modulus is constant at $1.487$ while the phase winds---emergent dual unitarity, prediction 4. Figure~\ref{fig:spectral_p0}b shows the central charge from the same eigenvalue: the corrected fit of Eq.~\eqref{eq:cft:eq3} returns $c=0.48$. The long window also settles the branch-constant question of Appendix~\ref{app:eq3}. Written for the unwrapped phase, the fit carries a constant $B$ alongside the Casimir coefficient, and on a short window the two trade off against each other. Left free over $T\le20$ the fit recovers $B=-3.141$ by itself, so at the integrable point they are no longer degenerate and the absolute read needs no pinning. The Casimir coefficient itself can be checked against an independent calculation: Bou-Comas \textit{et al.}~\cite{boucomas2026} give $\kappa/v=\pi/96=0.0327$ for the Ising chain, and we measure $|C|=0.0318$, a difference of $2.8\%$ from a separate implementation of the method.

\begin{figure}[htbp]

    \caption{\textbf{The spectral predictions at the integrable point, $T=2$--$20$.} (a) $\lambda_0$ in the complex plane: constant modulus, winding phase. (b) The corrected Eq.~\eqref{eq:cft:eq3} fit of the de-biased phase, giving $c=0.48$.}
    \label{fig:spectral_p0}
\end{figure}

The boundary exponent distinguishes the two boundary conditions of Section~\ref{sec:cft:temporal}. Fitting Eq.~\eqref{eq:cft:eq4} to the first spectral gap over $T=2$--$9$ gives $x_1=0.49$ for the free boundary ($\ket{X+}$) and $x_1=1.97$ for the fixed one ($\ket{Up}$), against the exact values $\tfrac12$ and $2$ (Figure~\ref{fig:x1_p0}).

    \caption{\textbf{The boundary exponent at the integrable point.} The first spectral gap against $T$ for the free and fixed boundary states with the fits of Eq.~\eqref{eq:cft:eq4}, giving $x_1=0.49$ and $1.97$ against the exact $\tfrac12$ and $2$.}
    \label{fig:x1_p0}
\end{figure}

Under frustration the spectral route survives where the entropy route has already failed. Figure~\ref{fig:partner_gap}b shows $|\lambda_0|(T)$ for every coupling: each arm sits on its own constant radius, so dual unitarity persists across the frustrated range. The measurements nevertheless become harder, and Figure~\ref{fig:partner_gap}a shows why. At every $p$ the leading eigenvalue acquires a companion of almost equal modulus, within one per cent of it from $T\approx4$ onwards. The approach is not accidental: the companion sits a boundary rung away, and every rung shrinks as $\pi x/(v\,T)$, so the pair must merge as $T$ grows. It is a member of the displaced family that Section~\ref{sec:results:tower} identifies, and it is this near-degeneracy that forces the phase-predicted tracking of Section~\ref{sec:numerics:practical}. At $p=0.1$ the tracking follows the physical branch to $T=11$; the companion in fact overtakes it in modulus from $T\approx6$, which is where a selector based on modulus alone would fail. At $p\ge0.3$ the branch is lost after a few rungs, so no eigenvalue central charge follows there yet.

(b) $|\lambda_0|(T)$ per coupling: each arm holds its own constant radius, emergent dual unitarity across the frustrated range.}

Figure~\ref{fig:eq3_p} shows the same fit at $p=0.1$. The branch constant again settles on $-\pi$ without being asked to, and over the same $T\le9$ window the measured velocity $v(0.1)=2.60$ gives $c=0.54$, which two independent ladders reproduce ($0.536$ from the eigenvalue-only arm, $0.572$ from the full-eigenvector one). Both are consistent with the Ising value at the accuracy the integrable point itself achieves, where the same construction returns $0.48$.

The choice of window matters, and $B$ is what exposes it. Extending the $p=0.1$ fit to $T\le11$ halves the Casimir coefficient and drops the read to $0.32$, while $B$ drifts from $-3.144$ to $-3.148$; pushing further still makes it change sign. The integrable point shows nothing of the kind. So the fit carries its own internal check: as long as the free $B$ sits at $-\pi$ the window is sound, and at $p=0.1$ it stops doing so exactly where the tracking begins to lose the physical eigenvalue. Table~\ref{tab:cp} collects the measured universal data.

\subsection{The momentum-\texorpdfstring{$\pi$}{pi} displaced boundary tower}
\label{sec:results:tower}

Read naively, the boundary-exponent measurement fails at $p\neq0$. Taking the subleading eigenvalue nearest $\lambda_0$ in phase, as at the integrable point, Eq.~\eqref{eq:cft:eq4} returns $x_1\approx\tfrac32$ at $p=0.1$---a heavier operator than the free-boundary $\tfrac12$, as if the lightest state had disappeared from the spectrum.

The deep $k=8$ spectra show where it went. Figure~\ref{fig:tower} places the block members in the plane of phase gap to $\lambda_0$ versus relative modulus. One family sits at small phase gaps, on the even-parity identity ladder $x=2,3,4$. A second enters from phase $\pi$, on the odd-parity ladder $x=\tfrac12,\tfrac32,\tfrac52$ read from that side. Nothing is missing---the entire odd ladder, which contains the lightest boundary operator, is displaced in phase by momentum $\pi$. A state on that side is therefore read with the gap measured from $\pi$ rather than from zero, $x = v\,T\,(\pi-|\Delta\varphi|)/\pi$, and one on the near side with Eq.~\eqref{eq:cft:eq4} as usual. Reading each eigenvalue on its own side restores $x_1=0.49$ at $p=0$ and $x_1=0.47$ at $p=0.1$, the free-boundary value at both couplings.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{imgs/res_tower_spectrum.png}
    \caption{\textbf{The two boundary ladders in the deep spectrum.} The $k=8$ block at $p=0.1$ in the plane of phase gap to $\lambda_0$ versus relative modulus, for three evolution times. Blue: even (identity) ladder at the guides $x=2,3,4$; red: odd (boundary-fermion) ladder at the guides $x=\tfrac12,\tfrac32,\tfrac52$ entering from the $\pi$ side.}
    \label{fig:tower}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:tower_p} shows the same reading along the eigenvalue ladders. At $p=0$ the full free-boundary ladder $\{\tfrac12,\tfrac32,2\}$ sits near $\lambda_0$ and holds flat from $T=2$ to $20$; there is no displaced family, as expected for free fermions. At $p=0.1$ the ladder splits: the identity operator $x=2$ stays near $\lambda_0$ while the odd operators $\{\tfrac12,\tfrac32\}$ appear on the $\pi$ side, flat in $T$ up to $T\approx11$. Turning on the interaction is what moves the odd ladder. The displacement follows from the parity-dressed hopping of Section~\ref{sec:annni:jw}, where the \ac{NNN} term attaches a momentum $\pi$ to the odd-fermion-parity sector. Appendix~\ref{app:pairing} derives it and explains why a free-fermion chain cannot produce it. At $p\ge0.3$ the reading is not yet conclusive. The subleading block members are the least converged, and within the short trackable window the two families are no longer cleanly separated, so those couplings are left to the finer ladders.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{imgs/res_tower_across_p.png}
    \caption{\textbf{The displaced tower appears with the interaction.} Boundary dimensions read from the eigenvalue ladders with the measured $v(p)$, blue for states near $\lambda_0$ and red for states read from the $\pi$ side; dashed lines mark $x=\tfrac12,\tfrac32,2,3$. At $p=0$ the whole ladder sits near $\lambda_0$; at $p=0.1$ the odd operators have moved to the $\pi$ side and stay flat in $T$.}
    \label{fig:tower_p}
\end{figure}

The two ladders also explain why frustration makes the extraction harder. Every boundary dimension is read from a phase gap, and Eq.~\eqref{eq:cft:eq4} fixes that gap at $\pi x/(v\,T)$. The velocity therefore sets the scale on which the spectrum is spread out. Raising $p$ from $0$ to $0.5$ raises $v$ from $1.98$ to $4.93$, so the same operators are compressed into a phase window two and a half times narrower, and the displaced ladder puts a second family inside it. Resolving them asks more of the solver precisely where the block is least well conditioned. This is the origin of every limitation reported above: the shrinking windows, the earlier wall, and the tracking that fails at $p\ge0.3$. The universal content, wherever it can be measured, remains that of the Ising boundary \ac{CFT}; what frustration changes is how much of it stays resolvable.
