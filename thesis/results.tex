\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

The four predictions of Section~\ref{sec:cft:temporal} are now confronted with the simulations. The logic of this section follows the two families of measurements those predictions define. The entropy route goes through the eigenvectors: the generalized R\'enyi-2 entropy of Equation~\eqref{eq:s2gen}, built from the two dominant boundary states, is compared with the conformal profile of Equation~\eqref{eq:cft:chord}. The spectral route goes through the eigenvalues alone: emergent dual unitarity, the central charge from the phase curvature of $\lambda_0$ (Equation~\eqref{eq:cft:eq3}), and the boundary exponent from the first gap (Equation~\eqref{eq:cft:eq4}). We first reproduce all of these results at the integrable point, replicating the benchmark of Carignano and Tagliacozzo~\cite{carignano_tagliacozzo2025} (Section~\ref{sec:results:validation}). We then switch on frustration: the entropy route delivers the headline central charge and then breaks at a well-defined wall, whose diagnosis ties directly to the figures of merit of Section~\ref{sec:reach} (Section~\ref{sec:results:wall}); the spectral route survives further, and reading it correctly uncovers a genuine feature of the interacting spectrum---a boundary tower displaced by momentum $\pi$---that resolves the boundary-exponent measurement and reshapes how the numerical convergence degrades with $p$ (Section~\ref{sec:results:spectral}). We close by mapping where the reach of the method ends (Section~\ref{sec:results:limits}).

Unless stated otherwise, all runs use the second-order VD2 time-evolution MPO (Section~\ref{sec:mporep}) with $\delta t=0.05$--$0.1$, a conformal regulator $\beta_0=0.2$ ($N_\beta=4$ imaginary-time sites split between the two ends, Section~\ref{sec:cft:temporal}), the initial state $\ket{\Psi_0}=\ket{X+}^{\otimes N}$, and the block power method of Section~\ref{sec:numerics} to obtain the dominant temporal states. All eigenvalue-based measurements use the measured sound velocity $v(p)$ of Section~\ref{sec:annni:velocity}.

\subsection{Validation at the integrable point}
\label{sec:results:validation}

At $p=0$ the model is the transverse-field Ising chain, every conformal prediction has an exact target, and the transfer matrix is left--right symmetric, so this point calibrates the pipeline and fixes conventions. We reproduce here the full set of results established for this chain by Carignano and Tagliacozzo~\cite{carignano_tagliacozzo2025}.

The entropy route comes first. Figure~\ref{fig:val_profiles} shows the generalized temporal entropy across every temporal cut of the converged tMPS, for several evolution times. The real part traces the symmetric conformal dome of Equation~\eqref{eq:cft:chord}, its peak growing logarithmically with $T$, and lies on the $c=\tfrac12$ chord; fitting the slope returns $c=0.518,\,0.529,\,0.509$ at $T=4,8,12$. The imaginary part sits on the predicted plateau $\pi c/12$: the measured mid-cut values approach $0.1309$, to be compared with $\pi/24\approx0.1309$---a fit-free read of the central charge from the same data.

The spectral route reproduces the remaining predictions. Figure~\ref{fig:val_circle} (left) traces the leading eigenvalue $\lambda_0(T)$ in the complex plane: the points sit on a circle of near-constant radius while the phase winds---emergent dual unitarity, prediction 4. The radius is $|\lambda_0|\approx1.497$ rather than one because its absolute value depends on the per-column normalization of the transfer matrix (through it, $|\lambda_0|$ sets the Loschmidt rate of Equation~\eqref{eq:rate}); the dual-unitarity signature is that this radius becomes constant in $T$, while only the phase keeps evolving. Figure~\ref{fig:val_circle} (right) shows the central-charge extraction from the same eigenvalue, Equation~\eqref{eq:cft:eq3}: after removing the constant branch offset $B=-\pi$ (the subtlety discussed in Appendix~\ref{app:eq3}), the de-biased phase divided by $T$ exposes the Casimir curvature, and the fit returns $c=0.4997$. Finally, Figure~\ref{fig:val_x1} shows the boundary-exponent measurement, Equation~\eqref{eq:cft:eq4}, for the two boundary conditions of Section~\ref{sec:cft:temporal}: the free state gives $x_1=0.503$ (with leading coefficient $a_1=0.789$, essentially the exact $\pi/4$), the fixed state $x_1=2.025$---both within one percent of the exact boundary-CFT values $\tfrac12$ and $2$. Every prediction of Section~\ref{sec:cft:temporal} is thus recovered at the integrable point, and the pipeline is calibrated.

\subsection{Switching on frustration: the entropy route and its wall}
\label{sec:results:wall}

With the pipeline calibrated, we tune the frustration away from the integrable point and repeat the entropy measurement. Through a well-defined window the dome remains conformal: extracting the R\'enyi-2 slope over $T=4$--$9$ at $p=0.1$, and calibrating against $p=0$ so that the non-universal finite-$T$, finite-$\chi$ and finite-$\delta t$ prefactors cancel in the ratio, gives
\begin{equation}
    c(p=0.1) = 0.47 \pm 0.05,
    \label{eq:results:headline}
\end{equation}
with per-time values $0.43,\,0.46,\,0.56,\,0.48,\,0.46,\,0.42$---consistent with the Ising value $c=\tfrac12$. The fit-free imaginary plateau corroborates it independently, sitting near $\tfrac{12}{\pi}\,\mathrm{Im}\,S_2\approx0.55$ across the same window. Temporal Ising universality, by this route, survives the breaking of integrability.

The same verification extends to stronger frustration. The warm-started cluster sweep provides converged boundary states at $p=0.3$ and $p=0.5$ within their own clean windows---windows that shrink as $p$ grows, for the reasons quantified in Section~\ref{sec:results:limits}---and their domes and calibrated slopes are analyzed in exactly the same way as at $p=0.1$. \textit{[OPEN: Figure~\ref{fig:frust_domes} is reserved for the $p=0.3$ and $p=0.5$ domes and their calibrated central charges; the analysis pipeline is in place and the figure fills in once the cluster arms complete.]}

The entropy route, however, has a hard ceiling, and Figure~\ref{fig:wall} shows it from both sides. The left panel shows the dome itself: conformal at $T=6$ and $T=8$, and then inflated discontinuously at $T=10$, its peak jumping by more than a third in a single step. The right panel shows why, using the figure of merit introduced in Section~\ref{sec:reach}: the phase rigidity $r$ of the dominant eigenvector pair (Equation~\eqref{eq:rigidity}) collapses geometrically---five orders of magnitude across the sweep---and reaches its floor exactly where the dome breaks. The transfer matrix is approaching an exceptional point: the left and right dominant eigenvectors are coalescing, the individual eigenvector is becoming ill-conditioned at the rate $1/\mathrm{gap}$ anticipated by Equation~\eqref{eq:reach}, and any entropy built from it loses meaning. The eigenvalues, tellingly, remain accurate through the wall---checked against exact diagonalization to $10^{-8}$--$10^{-13}$---so this is not a failure of the solver but of the object being asked for. Appendix~\ref{app:wall} collects the checks behind this diagnosis: a short account of what an exceptional point is and how it degrades the eigenvectors, the verification that no post-processing of the converged subspace rescues the entropy past this point, and the demonstration that the wall is gap-limited rather than resolution-limited---halving the bond dimension, tightening the truncation cutoff, and switching from RTM to RDM truncation all leave its onset at the same $T$. Past the wall, the physics must be read from the eigenvalues, which is exactly what the next subsection does.

\subsection{The spectral route: dual unitarity, the displaced tower, and the boundary exponent}
\label{sec:results:spectral}

The eigenvalue measurements survive frustration, and reading them carefully turns out to be rewarding: the boundary-exponent measurement led us to a genuine feature of the interacting spectrum.

Emergent dual unitarity persists at $p\neq0$. Figure~\ref{fig:frust_circle} traces $\lambda_0(T)$ for both couplings: each sits on its own circle of near-constant radius ($|\lambda_0|\approx1.489$ at $p=0$, $1.549$ at $p=0.1$) with a winding phase. The transfer matrix still becomes effectively unitary; frustration does not spoil the conformal structure, exactly as prediction 4 requires for a critical quench.

The value of each radius reflects the per-column normalization and the Loschmidt rate; the dual-unitarity signature is that both radii stay constant in $T$ while the phases wind.}
    \label{fig:frust_circle}

The central charge from the eigenvalue route confirms the entropy result. Figure~\ref{fig:frust_eq3} shows the corrected Equation~\eqref{eq:cft:eq3} fit for both couplings on the pre-wall window: calibrating the $1/T$ coefficient against $p=0$---the same ratio construction as the entropy, cancelling the velocity and every shared prefactor---returns $c=0.52$ at $p=0.1$. The absolute (uncalibrated) read at $p=0.1$ is not yet stable, and the reason is worth recording. \textit{[Working-draft note, pending the longer cluster windows: the corrected fit takes the form $\mathrm{Im}\,\lambda_0 = A\,T + B + C/T + D/T^3$, where $B$ is the constant branch offset and $C$ carries the Casimir term that measures $c$. Over a short window in $T$, a constant and a slowly varying $1/T$ term are nearly indistinguishable: the fit can trade $B$ against $C$ at almost no cost in the residual, so the absolute value of $C$ is poorly determined even though the calibrated ratio---in which the bias cancels---remains stable. Pinning $B=-\pi$ (Appendix~\ref{app:eq3}) removes the degeneracy exactly at the integrable point, but at $p\neq0$ a residual freedom still biases $|C|$ on windows limited to $T\lesssim9$. The longer ladders of the running cluster campaign, reaching $T\approx14$--$20$, separate the two directions of the fit and will allow an absolute read of $c(p)$.]}

\label{sec:results:tower}
The boundary exponent is where the spectrum demanded to be understood. Read naively---taking the subleading eigenvalue nearest $\lambda_0$ in phase, as at the integrable point---Equation~\eqref{eq:cft:eq4} returns $x_1\approx\tfrac32$ at $p=0.1$: a heavier operator than the free-boundary $\tfrac12$, which at a first reading one might take as anomalous. The resolution lies in the structure of the free-boundary Ising spectrum, which consists of two interleaved ladders: the even-parity identity module, with boundary dimensions $\{0,2,3,4,\dots\}$, and the odd-parity boundary-fermion module, with $\{\tfrac12,\tfrac32,\tfrac52,\dots\}$---the lightest operator, $x_1=\tfrac12$, belonging to the odd ladder. At $p=0$ both ladders sit at small phase next to $\lambda_0$. For any $p\neq0$, however, the entire odd-parity ladder is displaced in phase by momentum $\pi$. This is precisely the dynamical consequence of the parity-dressed hopping identified in Section~\ref{sec:annni:jw}: the four-fermion NNN term attaches a momentum shift of $\pi$ to the odd-fermion-parity sector, so the odd states appear on the far side of the spectrum, at phase gaps near $\pi$ rather than near zero (the full derivation, including why free-fermion chains cannot produce this displacement, is given in Appendix~\ref{app:pairing}).

Figure~\ref{fig:tower} makes this structure visible directly. It shows the deep $k=8$ block at $p=0.1$, $T=4$ in the plane of phase gap versus relative modulus: the eigenvalues organize into the even ladder near phase zero---reading $x\approx1.93,\,3.0,\,4.1$, the identity module---and a second family at phase gaps near $\pi$, at nearly full modulus, whose dimensions read from the $\pi$ side, $x=v(p)\,T\,(\pi-|\Delta\varphi|)/\pi$, are $x\approx0.47,\,1.44$: the odd ladder, displaced but intact. Table~\ref{tab:tower} confirms the assignment across evolution times. Nothing is missing from the spectrum, and nothing is anomalous: the lightest boundary operator simply sits at momentum $\pi$, invisible to an estimator that only looks near $\lambda_0$. A partner-inclusive reading---assigning each eigenvalue the dimension $|\Delta\varphi|$ or $(\pi-|\Delta\varphi|)$, whichever is consistent, and taking the smallest---restores
\begin{equation}
    x_1(p{=}0) = 0.49, \qquad x_1(p{=}0.1) = 0.47,
    \label{eq:results:x1}
\end{equation}
the free-boundary value at both couplings. The $x_1\approx\tfrac32$ of the naive reading was the even module's first excited state, measured in place of the displaced $\tfrac12$.

The displaced tower also reshapes how the numerical convergence degrades with frustration, because the near-degeneracies that matter to the method are no longer only the conformal compression of a single ladder. Two definitions must be separated. The physical gap that enters prediction 3 is the gap within the tower of $\lambda_0$,
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{gap}(T) = \frac{\max\{|\theta_i| : i\in\text{tower},\ i\neq i_0\}}{|\theta_{i_0}|},
  \label{eq:physgap}
\end{equation}
excluding the displaced states; the naive ratio of the two largest moduli instead measures the near-degeneracy of $\lambda_0$ with its nearest displaced partner, which approaches one much earlier. This distinction is also what dictated the branch-selection rule of Section~\ref{sec:numerics:practical}: the displaced partner sits at nearly the same modulus as $\lambda_0$ but far away in phase, so a selector anchored on complex-plane proximity drifts onto it as the phase of $\lambda_0$ winds---the failure that modulus dominance repairs. Figure~\ref{fig:pgap} shows the tower gap of Equation~\eqref{eq:physgap} against $T$ for every frustration in the sweep: at each $p$ the gap closes monotonically toward one---the conformal degeneracy building up---and it does so at ever smaller $T$ as $p$ grows, both because the larger $v(p)$ compresses the phase ladder (Section~\ref{sec:annni:velocity}) and because the displaced tower crowds the leading block. The reach of the method shrinks accordingly, which is the subject of the final subsection.

\subsection{The frustration sweep and the limits of the method}
\label{sec:results:limits}

\textit{[OPEN: the full sweep is a set of parallel, warm-started and checkpointed block-solver jobs on the BSC cluster, spanning $p\in\{0,0.1,0.3,0.5,0.8,1.0,1.5\}$; at the time of writing the low-frustration arms are clean and the higher ones are still landing or being re-run at larger block size, so Table~\ref{tab:cp} is filled where the data is trustworthy and left open otherwise.]}

The provisional picture separates two statements that the sweep will finish quantifying. The universal data---the central charge and the boundary exponent---remain at their Ising values wherever the measurements are clean, currently through $p=0.5$. The reach over which they can be measured, by contrast, shrinks steadily with $p$: the trustworthy window extends to $T\approx12$ at $p=0$, $T\approx8$ at $p=0.1$, $T\approx7$ at $p=0.3$ and $p=0.5$, and collapses beyond. At $p=0.8$ the leading block at the production size is dominated by displaced-tower states and requires the escalated block size of Section~\ref{sec:numerics:practical} to resolve the physical gap at all. At $p\ge1.0$ something qualitatively different happens: the top of the spectrum acquires a near-exact degeneracy---two eigenvalues equal to machine precision---which the solver cannot resolve, and the physical branch is lost entirely. This is not a memory or truncation limit but a genuine method wall at strong frustration, of the same kind that an exact symmetry degeneracy of the quench produces.

Two further checks complete the map of the limits. First, the wall of Section~\ref{sec:results:wall} is set by the physics, not the resolution: the bond dimension, the truncation cutoff, and the truncation scheme all leave its onset unchanged, and the phase rigidity collapses at the same rate under all of them. Second, the RTM-versus-RDM comparison of Section~\ref{sec:transverse:truncation} is settled empirically: where both converge they agree on every eigenvalue and on the rigidity to four to five digits, so the two schemes are physically equivalent---and since the RDM truncation costs several times more (up to an order of magnitude at large $T$) for no gain in reach, the gauge-invariant RTM truncation is the right production choice, with its milder convergence monitored rather than feared.

The overall picture is the asymmetry of Equation~\eqref{eq:reach} realized in data. The spectrum of the transfer matrix is robust deep into the dual-unitary regime, and it carries all four conformal predictions through the frustrated range; the temporal entropy, which needs an individual eigenvector, is trustworthy only in the pre-wall window where the dominant pair is still isolated. The universal physics---the central charge, the boundary content, the dual-unitary circle---is extracted precisely in that window, and the wall that closes it is the price the method pays for the very conformal structure it exploits.
