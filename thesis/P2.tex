\section{The ANNNI-type Model}
\label{sec:annni}

We study a self-dual quantum spin chain that generalizes the transverse-field Ising model by adding next-nearest-neighbour (NNN) interactions \cite{alcaraz2016}. For the spin-$1/2$ case its Hamiltonian reads
\begin{equation}
    H = - \sum_{i} \left( \sigma_i^z \sigma_{i+1}^z
        + p\, \sigma_i^z \sigma_{i+2}^z
        + \lambda\, \sigma_i^x
        + p\lambda\, \sigma_i^x \sigma_{i+1}^x \right),
    \label{eq:annni_H}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma^{x,z}$ are the standard Pauli matrices, $\lambda$ is the transverse field, and $p$ controls the strength of the longer-range (NNN) coupling.

Setting $p=0$ removes the NNN terms and recovers the standard, integrable transverse-field Ising model, with $\lambda$ playing the role of the transverse magnetic field. Switching on $p>0$ introduces a second interaction length scale and breaks integrability: the chain acquires genuine many-body chaos and is no longer exactly solvable.

Breaking integrability raises the following question: does the model remain critical, and if so, does it stay in the same universality class as the Ising chain? We answer this in equilibrium by examining the ground-state entanglement and comparing it with the predictions of Conformal Field Theory (Section~\ref{sec:cft}).

For a critical 1D system, CFT predicts that the ground-state von Neumann entropy of a block of length $l$ in an open chain of length $L$ scales logarithmically as (the open-chain, half-line version of Equation~\eqref{eq:cft:coeff} at $n\to1$)
\begin{equation}
    S_{\mathrm{vN}}(l,L) = \frac{c}{6}\,
        \ln\!\left(\frac{L}{\pi}\sin\!\frac{l\pi}{L}\right) + k,
    \label{eq:cft_obc}
\end{equation}
where $c$ is the central charge of the underlying CFT and $k$ is a non-universal constant. Evaluating Eq.~\eqref{eq:cft_obc} at the mid-chain cut $l=L/2$, where $\sin(\pi/2)=1$, simplifies it to
\begin{equation}
    S_{\mathrm{vN}}(L/2) = \frac{c}{6}\,\ln\!\left(\frac{L}{\pi}\right) + k
        = \frac{c}{6}\,\ln L + k'.
    \label{eq:cft_mid}
\end{equation}
Thus, if we run DMRG for a range of system sizes, extract the mid-bond von Neumann entropy, and plot it against $\ln L$, criticality manifests as a straight line whose slope is exactly $c/6$.

    \label{fig:cft_L}
\end{figure}

As Figure~\ref{fig:cft_L} shows, the mid-chain entropy at $p=0.5$ follows precisely this linear law, confirming that, even with integrability broken, the system still exhibits the scale invariance characteristic of a CFT.

We now ask how the central charge evolves as the NNN coupling is increased. In his original proposal \cite{alcaraz2016}, Alcaraz argued, via finite-size scaling, that for sufficiently small coupling ($p\lesssim1.5$) the model stays critical and in the same universality class as the integrable Ising chain, so the central charge should remain very close to $c=1/2$.


\begin{figure}[h!]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{imgs/p_dependence.png}
    \caption{Dependence of the fitted central charge $c$ on the frustration parameter $p$ for the Alcaraz model and a finite chain of $N=300$ spins. The values remain close to the Ising prediction $c=1/2$ over the studied range.}
    \label{fig:c_vs_p}
\end{figure}

To verify this numerically, the central charge was fitted across $p\in[0,2]$ on a chain of $N=300$ spins (Figure~\ref{fig:c_vs_p}). At the integrable point $p=0$ the extracted value is $c\approx0.502$. The small deviation from the exact $c=1/2$ is a well-understood finite-size artifact: on a finite lattice, the subleading corrections to Eq.~\eqref{eq:cft_obc} have not fully vanished, producing the systematic offset observed from the thermodynamic limit. As $p$ grows, the fitted charge drifts gently upward, but the deviation remains small---even at $p=2$, it barely exceeds $0.510$. This persistent proximity to $c=1/2$ provides strong numerical support for Alcaraz's claim \cite{alcaraz2016}: the NNN coupling does not drive the system into a new phase. The model remains critical and within the Ising universality class, so the CFT framework is a legitimate tool for describing its dynamics.

This equilibrium picture also fixes the role that the integrable point $p=0$ will play in the dynamical study that follows. At $p=0$ the model \emph{is} the transverse-field Ising chain, whose post-quench temporal entropies were computed from CFT and reproduced numerically by Carignano and Tagliacozzo~\cite{carignano_tagliacozzo2025}. It is therefore our touchstone: a point where the exact conformal answer is known, against which we can calibrate the whole transverse pipeline before switching on frustration. The novelty of this work is then to \emph{tune} $p$ away from that solvable point---keeping the model critical and Ising-class, as we have just verified, but non-integrable and longer-ranged---and ask whether the same universal signatures persist.

Once the equilibrium criticality is established, we move to the dynamical question that motivates the rest of this work. We quench the system from the polarized initial state $\ket{\Psi_0}=\ket{X+}^{\otimes N}$ to the critical point $\lambda=1$ and ask whether the universal CFT signatures that organize the equilibrium entanglement also organize the temporal entanglement of the post-quench dynamics. To address this, we turn to transverse contraction, which gives us efficient access to the generalized temporal entropies whose CFT predictions we wish to test.
