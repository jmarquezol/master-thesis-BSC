\section{The ANNNI-type Model}
\label{sec:annni}

We study a self-dual quantum spin chain that generalizes the transverse-field Ising model by adding next-nearest-neighbour (NNN) interactions \cite{alcaraz2016}. For the spin-$1/2$ case its Hamiltonian reads
\begin{equation}
    H = - \sum_{i} \left( \sigma_i^z \sigma_{i+1}^z
        + p\, \sigma_i^z \sigma_{i+2}^z
        + \lambda\, \sigma_i^x
        + p\lambda\, \sigma_i^x \sigma_{i+1}^x \right),
    \label{eq:annni_H}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma^{x,z}$ are the standard Pauli matrices, $\lambda$ is the transverse field, and $p$ controls the strength of the longer-range (NNN) coupling.

Setting $p=0$ removes the NNN terms and recovers the standard, integrable transverse-field Ising model, with $\lambda$ playing the role of the transverse magnetic field. Switching on $p>0$ introduces a second interaction length scale and breaks integrability: the chain acquires genuine many-body chaos and is no longer exactly solvable.

\subsection{Equilibrium criticality and the central charge}
\label{sec:annni:equilibrium}

Breaking integrability raises the following question: does the model remain critical, and if so, does it stay in the same universality class as the Ising chain? We answer this in equilibrium by examining the ground-state entanglement and comparing it with the predictions of Conformal Field Theory (Section~\ref{sec:cft}).

For a critical 1D system, CFT predicts that the ground-state von Neumann entropy of a block of length $l$ in an open chain of length $L$ scales logarithmically as (the open-chain, half-line version of Equation~\eqref{eq:cft:coeff} at $n\to1$)
\begin{equation}
    S_{\mathrm{vN}}(l,L) = \frac{c}{6}\,
        \ln\!\left(\frac{L}{\pi}\sin\!\frac{l\pi}{L}\right) + k,
    \label{eq:cft_obc}
\end{equation}
where $c$ is the central charge of the underlying CFT and $k$ is a non-universal constant. Evaluating Eq.~\eqref{eq:cft_obc} at the mid-chain cut $l=L/2$, where $\sin(\pi/2)=1$, simplifies it to
\begin{equation}
    S_{\mathrm{vN}}(L/2) = \frac{c}{6}\,\ln\!\left(\frac{L}{\pi}\right) + k
        = \frac{c}{6}\,\ln L + k'.
    \label{eq:cft_mid}
\end{equation}
Thus, if we run DMRG for a range of system sizes, extract the mid-bond von Neumann entropy, and plot it against $\ln L$, criticality manifests as a straight line whose slope is exactly $c/6$.

    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{imgs/cft_L.png}
    \caption{Finite-size scaling of the mid-chain von Neumann entropy for the ANNNI-type model at $p=0.5$. The linear dependence on $\ln L$ is consistent with the CFT prediction of Eq.~\eqref{eq:cft_mid}, and from the slope we obtain the central charge $c$.}
    \label{fig:cft_L}
\end{figure}

As Figure~\ref{fig:cft_L} shows, the mid-chain entropy at $p=0.5$ follows precisely this linear law, confirming that, even with integrability broken, the system still exhibits the scale invariance characteristic of a CFT.

We now ask how the central charge evolves as the NNN coupling is increased. In his original proposal \cite{alcaraz2016}, Alcaraz argued, via finite-size scaling, that for sufficiently small coupling ($p\lesssim1.5$) the model stays critical and in the same universality class as the integrable Ising chain, so the central charge should remain very close to $c=1/2$.


\begin{figure}[h!]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{imgs/p_dependence.png}
    \caption{Dependence of the fitted central charge $c$ on the frustration parameter $p$ for the Alcaraz model and a finite chain of $N=300$ spins. The values remain close to the Ising prediction $c=1/2$ over the studied range.}
    \label{fig:c_vs_p}
\end{figure}

To verify this numerically, the central charge was fitted across $p\in[0,2]$ on a chain of $N=300$ spins (Figure~\ref{fig:c_vs_p}). At the integrable point $p=0$ the extracted value is $c\approx0.502$. The small deviation from the exact $c=1/2$ is a well-understood finite-size artifact: on a finite lattice, the subleading corrections to Eq.~\eqref{eq:cft_obc} have not fully vanished, producing the systematic offset observed from the thermodynamic limit. As $p$ grows, the fitted charge drifts gently upward, but the deviation remains small---even at $p=2$, it barely exceeds $0.510$. This persistent proximity to $c=1/2$ provides strong numerical support for Alcaraz's claim \cite{alcaraz2016}: the NNN coupling does not drive the system into a new phase. The model remains critical and within the Ising universality class, so the CFT framework is a legitimate tool for describing its dynamics.

This equilibrium picture also fixes the role that the integrable point $p=0$ will play in the dynamical study that follows. At $p=0$ the model is the transverse-field Ising chain, whose post-quench temporal entropies were computed from CFT and reproduced numerically by Carignano and Tagliacozzo~\cite{carignano_tagliacozzo2025}. It is therefore our touchstone: a point where the exact conformal answer is known, against which we can calibrate the whole transverse pipeline before switching on frustration. The novelty of this work is then to tune $p$ away from that solvable point---keeping the model critical and Ising-class, as we have just verified, but non-integrable and longer-ranged---and ask whether the same universal signatures persist.

\subsection{Why the NNN coupling breaks integrability}
\label{sec:annni:jw}

The claim that $p>0$ breaks integrability can be made precise, and the fermionic language in which it becomes transparent will also explain a key feature of the transfer-matrix spectrum later on. The \ac{JW} transformation rewrites the spin chain exactly as a chain of spinless fermions: in the convention of Equation~\eqref{eq:annni_H}, with the field on $\sigma^x$ and the order parameter on $\sigma^z$,
\begin{equation}
    \sigma^x_j = 1 - 2n_j, \qquad
    \sigma^z_j = \big(c_j + c_j^\dagger\big)\,K_j, \qquad
    K_j = \prod_{k<j}\big(1-2n_k\big),
    \label{eq:annni:jwmap}
\end{equation}
where $c_j$ are fermionic annihilation operators, $n_j=c^\dagger_j c_j$, and the string $K_j$ supplies the signs that make the fermions anticommute. Under this map the two Ising terms stay quadratic: the transverse field becomes a chemical potential, and the strings of the nearest-neighbour bond cancel between adjacent sites, leaving ordinary hopping and pairing terms. The $p=0$ chain is therefore a free-fermion theory---the reason it is exactly solvable, and the reason its critical point is described by a free ($c=\tfrac12$) Majorana CFT.

The two terms proportional to $p$ behave differently: both become products of four fermion operators, that is, genuine interactions. The self-dual $XX$ term turns into a density--density coupling, $(1-2n_j)(1-2n_{j+1})$, which is diagonal in the occupation basis and moves nothing. The NNN bond is more interesting: its strings no longer cancel completely, and one factor survives at the intermediate site,
\begin{equation}
    \sigma^z_i \sigma^z_{i+2}
    = \big(c_i^\dagger - c_i\big)\,\big(1-2n_{i+1}\big)\,\big(c_{i+2}+c_{i+2}^\dagger\big),
    \label{eq:annni:hop}
\end{equation}
a hop from site $i$ to site $i+2$ carrying a sign fixed by the occupation of the site skipped over---a parity-dressed hopping. With quartic terms present the fermions scatter, Wick's theorem no longer closes the correlation functions, and no free-fermion solution exists: the model is genuinely interacting for any $p\neq0$. Beyond breaking integrability, the parity-dressed hop has a specific dynamical consequence---it attaches a momentum shift of $\pi$ to the odd-fermion-parity sector of the transfer matrix---which we will meet again when reading the spectrum in Section~\ref{sec:results}. The full derivation is given in Appendix~\ref{app:pairing}.

\subsection{The sound velocity grows with frustration}
\label{sec:annni:velocity}

A second effect of the NNN coupling, less obvious than the breaking of integrability, is that it changes how fast information propagates. At a critical point the low-energy dispersion is linear, $\omega = v|k|$, and the sound velocity $v$ enters every conformal prediction of Section~\ref{sec:cft}: the phases of the transfer-matrix eigenvalues scale as $\pi x/(vT)$, so converting a measured phase into a scaling dimension requires knowing $v$. For the integrable chain the exact value is $v=2$; for $p>0$ it must be measured.

We extract $v(p)$ from the equilibrium dispersion, diagonalizing the periodic chain exactly at small sizes and reading the velocity off the lowest momentum-carrying excitation. The construction and its validation---including the reproduction of Alcaraz's published central charge through his own Casimir formula, fed with our measured velocities---are given in Appendix~\ref{app:velocity}. The result, shown in Figure~\ref{fig:velocity}, is a velocity that grows quickly with frustration:
\begin{equation}
    v(0)=1.98, \qquad v(0.1)=2.60, \qquad v(0.3)=3.80, \qquad v(0.5)=4.93.
    \label{eq:annni:vp}
\end{equation}
Physically, the NNN bond opens a second channel for correlations to spread---two sites per step instead of one---so the light cone widens and information is scrambled faster as $p$ grows. For the temporal analysis this has two consequences that will recur. First, every eigenvalue-based measurement must use the true $v(p)$, not the Ising value: with the wrong velocity, the spectral ladders of Section~\ref{sec:results} are misread. Second, at fixed evolution time $T$ a larger velocity compresses the phase ladder $\pi x/(vT)$, pushing the leading spectrum toward degeneracy earlier---a fact that returns when we discuss the reach of the method.

Once the equilibrium picture is established, we move to the dynamical question that motivates the rest of this work. We quench the system from the polarized initial state $\ket{\Psi_0}=\ket{X+}^{\otimes N}$ to the critical point $\lambda=1$ and ask whether the universal CFT signatures that organize the equilibrium entanglement also organize the temporal entanglement of the post-quench dynamics. To address this, we turn to transverse contraction, which gives us efficient access to the generalized temporal entropies whose CFT predictions we wish to test.
