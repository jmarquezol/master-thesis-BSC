\section{The ANNNI-type Model}
\label{sec:annni}

The framework developed in Section~\ref{sec:cft} requires a quench to a critical point. We study a self-dual spin chain that extends the transverse-field Ising model with next-nearest-neighbour interactions~\cite{alcaraz2016}. For spin $1/2$, the Hamiltonian is
\begin{equation}
    H = - \sum_{i} \Big[ \sigma_i^z \sigma_{i+1}^z + \lambda\, \sigma_i^x
        \;+\; p\,\big( \sigma_i^z \sigma_{i+2}^z + \lambda\, \sigma_i^x \sigma_{i+1}^x \big) \Big],
    \label{eq:annni_H}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma^{x,z}$ are Pauli matrices, $\lambda$ is the transverse field, and $p$ controls the strength of the additional interactions. The first two terms define the transverse-field Ising chain. The terms proportional to $p$ introduce the \ac{NNN} bond and the accompanying $\sigma^x\sigma^x$ interaction required to preserve self-duality.

Throughout this work we set $\lambda=1$, where the chain is critical and the conformal predictions of Section~\ref{sec:cft} apply. At $p=0$, Equation~\eqref{eq:annni_H} reduces to the integrable transverse-field Ising chain. For $p>0$, the additional interactions introduce a second length scale and break the free-fermion structure, as shown in Section~\ref{sec:annni:jw}.

\subsection{Equilibrium criticality and the central charge}
\label{sec:annni:equilibrium}

From finite-size scaling, one can show that the chain remains critical and in the Ising universality class up to $p\lesssim1.5$, with central charge $c \approx 1/2$~\cite{alcaraz2016}. The dynamics becomes progressively more difficult to simulate as $p$ increases, as discussed in Section~\ref{sec:results}. We therefore restrict our analysis to the window $0 \le p \le 0.5$.

We verify the equilibrium universality class over this range using the ground-state entanglement. For a critical open chain of length $L$, \ac{CFT} predicts that the von Neumann entropy of a block of length $l$ scales as~\cite{holzhey1994,vidal2003,calabrese_cardy2004}
\begin{equation}
    S_{\mathrm{vN}}(l,L) = \frac{c}{6}\,W(l,L) + k,
    \label{eq:cft_obc}
\end{equation}
where $W$ is the chord variable of Equation~\eqref{eq:chord} and $k$ is a non-universal constant. The von Neumann entropy is well suited to this equilibrium calculation because \ac{DMRG} provides directly the full Schmidt spectrum of the ground state---unlike the temporal calculation in Section~\ref{sec:transverse}, which requires evaluating a matrix logarithm---and its finite-size corrections are smaller than those of higher-order R\'enyi entropies.

For each value of $p$, we obtain the ground state of a chain with $N=100$ sites and evaluate the entropy across every bond. Equation~\eqref{eq:cft_obc} predicts a linear dependence on $W(l,L)$, with slope $c/6$. The complete entanglement profile of a single ground state therefore provides an estimate of the central charge. We fit the central half of the chain, where boundary corrections are smallest; the resulting profiles are shown in Figure~\ref{fig:cft_chord}.

\begin{figure}[h!]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.75\textwidth]{imgs/cft_chord_equilibrium.png}
    \caption{Ground-state entanglement profiles of the ANNNI-type chain at $\lambda=1$ and $N=100$ for $p=0$ to $0.5$ in steps of $0.1$. Each profile is linear in the chord variable $W$ of Equation~\eqref{eq:chord}, with slope $c/6$ as predicted by Equation~\eqref{eq:cft_obc}. The fitted central charge ranges from $0.5070$ to $0.5081$. Its small offset from $1/2$ is already present at $p=0$ and is attributed to finite-size corrections.}
    \label{fig:cft_chord}
\end{figure}

The profiles follow the conformal scaling law at every coupling. The fitted central charge remains between $0.5070$ and $0.5081$ over the full range, so it barely moves as $p$ grows. The common offset from the exact value $c=1/2$ is consistent with residual finite-size corrections to Equation~\eqref{eq:cft_obc}: it is present both at the exactly known point $p=0$ and at non-zero $p$. These results confirm that the additional interactions do not change the universality class over the range considered. Appendix~\ref{app:fss} provides an independent estimate from the finite-size scaling of the mid-chain entropy, together with a cross-check based on the R\'enyi-2 entropy.

The point $p=0$ provides the reference case for the dynamical analysis. In the transverse-field Ising chain, the post-quench temporal entropies have been derived from \ac{CFT} and reproduced numerically~\cite{carignano_tagliacozzo2025}. We use this exact limit to calibrate the transverse contraction in Section~\ref{sec:results:validation}, before introducing the non-integrable interactions.

\subsection{Fermionic form of the NNN interactions}
\label{sec:annni:jw}

The effect of the additional interactions becomes explicit after mapping the spin chain to fermions. In the convention of Equation~\eqref{eq:annni_H}, with the transverse field along $\sigma^x$ and the order parameter along $\sigma^z$, the \ac{JW} transformation~\cite{jordan_wigner1928,fradkin2013} is
\begin{equation}
    \sigma^x_j = 1 - 2n_j, \qquad
    \sigma^z_j = \big(c_j + c_j^\dagger\big)\,K_j, \qquad
    K_j = \prod_{k<j}\big(1-2n_k\big),
    \label{eq:annni:jwmap}
\end{equation}
where $c_j$ annihilates a spinless fermion, $n_j=c^\dagger_j c_j$, and the string $K_j$ enforces the fermionic anticommutation relations.

The two Ising terms remain quadratic under this transformation. The transverse field becomes a chemical-potential term, while the strings associated with the nearest-neighbour bond cancel and leave ordinary hopping and pairing. The $p=0$ chain is therefore a free-fermion model and can be diagonalised exactly~\cite{pfeuty1970}. At criticality, its continuum limit is the free Majorana \ac{CFT} with $c=\tfrac12$~\cite{difrancesco1997}.

The terms proportional to $p$ introduce quartic fermionic contributions. The self-dual $\sigma^x_j\sigma^x_{j+1}$ term becomes the density--density factor $(1-2n_j)(1-2n_{j+1})$, which is diagonal in the occupation basis. For the \ac{NNN} bond, the Jordan--Wigner strings do not cancel completely: a parity factor remains on the intermediate site,
\begin{equation}
    \sigma^z_i \sigma^z_{i+2}
    = \big(c_i^\dagger - c_i\big)\,\big(1-2n_{i+1}\big)\,\big(c_{i+2}+c_{i+2}^\dagger\big).
    \label{eq:annni:hop}
\end{equation}
This term describes hopping and pairing between sites $i$ and $i+2$, with an amplitude whose sign depends on the occupation of site $i+1$. We refer to this process as parity-dressed hopping. The quartic contributions allow the fermions to scatter, so Wick's theorem no longer reduces correlation functions to two-point functions~\cite{wick1950}. Consequently, the model is genuinely interacting and no longer admits a free-fermion solution for $p\neq0$. A detailed derivation is given in Appendix~\ref{app:pairing}.

The parity dependence also provides a microscopic origin for the sector structure observed in the transfer-matrix spectrum in Section~\ref{sec:results}. In particular, the additional interaction distinguishes fermionic sectors and produces a corresponding structure in the eigenvalues. The precise mechanism connecting the lattice interaction to the displaced boundary tower is not yet fully understood, and we return to this question in the conclusions.

\subsection{Sound velocity}
\label{sec:annni:velocity}

The \ac{NNN} interaction modifies the propagation of low-energy excitations. At the critical point, their linear dispersion defines the sound velocity $v$ through $\omega=v|k|$. This velocity enters the conformal predictions of Section~\ref{sec:cft}, so it must be determined independently before the dynamical data can be compared with them. For the integrable chain, $v=2$ exactly; for $p>0$, we determine it numerically.

We extract $v(p)$ from the equilibrium dispersion of small periodic chains by identifying the lowest excitation with non-zero momentum. The construction and its validation are described in Appendix~\ref{app:velocity}. As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:velocity}, the velocity increases monotonically with $p$ over the range studied. The additional interaction therefore changes a non-universal scale that must be included when testing the universal conformal predictions.

\end{figure}

Having established that the model remains in the Ising universality class and determined the coupling-dependent velocity, we now consider the quench from the completely polarised state. The next section develops the transverse-contraction framework used to test whether the conformal signatures persist for $p>0$.
