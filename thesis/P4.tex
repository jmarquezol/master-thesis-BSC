\section{Building the Time-Evolution MPO}
\label{sec:mporep}

For \ac{NN} Hamiltonians, the evolution operator $U(\delta t)=e^{-iH\delta t}$ is usually represented as a Trotter--Suzuki circuit of two-site gates, giving rise to the usual brick-wall structure~\cite{paeckel2019}. However, an interaction between sites $i$ and $i+2$ cannot be assigned to a single bond within this pattern. To preserve the one-site translation invariance required by the transverse contraction, as discussed in Section~\ref{sec:transverse:network}, we instead express the Hamiltonian as a \acf{FSM} and exponentiate the resulting representation.

\subsection{The Hamiltonian as a \texorpdfstring{\ac{FSM}}{FSM}}
\label{sec:mporep:fsm}

In the \ac{FSM} representation~\cite{crosswhite2008,pirvu2010}, the Hamiltonian is encoded as an \ac{MPO} whose local tensor has a block-upper-triangular form~\cite{parker2020}. Two identity blocks propagate the initial and final states, while the on-site block $D$ generates local terms. The block row $C$ initiates interactions, the block column $B$ terminates them, and the memory block $A$ propagates unfinished interactions between their endpoints. The virtual index therefore records whether an interaction has not yet begun, is being propagated, or has terminated. For more complicated Hamiltonians, this block structure can be generated automatically from the operator~\cite{hubig2017}. For the \ac{ANNNI}-type model of Eq.~\eqref{eq:annni_H}, the local tensor has bond dimension $D_W=5$ and takes the form
\begin{align}
  W_H & =
  \begin{pmatrix}
    \mathbb{1} & C & D          \\
    0          & A & B          \\
    0          & 0 & \mathbb{1}
  \end{pmatrix}=
  \begin{pmatrix}
    \mathbb{1} & -\sigma^z  & -p\lambda\,\sigma^x & -p\,\sigma^z & -\lambda\,\sigma^x \\
    0          & 0          & 0                   & 0            & \sigma^z           \\
    0          & 0          & 0                   & 0            & \sigma^x           \\
    0          & \mathbb{1} & 0                   & 0            & 0                  \\
    0          & 0          & 0                   & 0            & \mathbb{1}
  \end{pmatrix}.
  \label{eq:Wannni}
\end{align}

Figure~\ref{fig:fsm} represents Eq.~\eqref{eq:Wannni} through the allowed paths between the five virtual states. Each node denotes a value of the virtual index, while an arrow from state $a$ to state $b$, labelled by an operator $O$, represents the tensor entry $(W_H)_{ab}=O$. The \ac{FSM} moves from left to right along the chain, beginning in state $1$ and ending in state $5$.

  \label{fig:fsm}

The loop $1\to1$ applies identities until a Hamiltonian term is initiated. A direct transition $1\to5$ applies the on-site field and completes the term at the same site. An \ac{NN} interaction instead follows either $1\to2\to5$ or $1\to3\to5$: the first transition applies the operator at site $i$ and records whether a $\sigma^z$ or $\sigma^x$ endpoint remains to be applied; the second applies the corresponding operator at site $i+1$ and completes the interaction. The \ac{NNN} term follows $1\to4\to2\to5$: the first transition applies $\sigma^z$ at site $i$, the second inserts an identity at site $i+1$, and the third applies $\sigma^z$ at site $i+2$. After state $5$ is reached, the loop $5\to5$ applies identities along the remainder of the chain. Zero entries in $W_H$ correspond to absent arrows, so no other transitions are allowed.

Because the \ac{FSM} is directional, its local tensor is asymmetric under interchange of the virtual indices. The exponentiated operator inherits this structure, as does the spatial transfer matrix after moving to the transverse picture. The resulting operator $\mathcal{E}$ is generally non-Hermitian and non-normal, $\mathcal{E}\mathcal{E}^\dagger\neq\mathcal{E}^\dagger\mathcal{E}$. Its left and right eigenvectors are therefore not related by Hermitian conjugation, and we compute the two temporal boundary states separately.

\subsection{Exponentiating the \texorpdfstring{\ac{FSM}}{FSM}}
\label{sec:mporep:exp}

We seek an approximation $W(\tau)\approx e^{\tau H}$, with $\tau=-i\delta t$, built from the blocks of $W_H$. Because our Hamiltonian carries interactions at two different ranges, we use the construction of Van Damme et al.~\cite{vandamme2024}, which organises the expansion by the spatial overlap of the local Hamiltonian terms. Contributions with disjoint support factorise and are kept to all orders, so only connected clusters of overlapping terms have to be reproduced explicitly. Appendix~\ref{app:mpo:vd2} shows how the operator is built from the machine of Figure~\ref{fig:fsm}.

We use its second-order member, denoted \textit{VD2}, whose virtual bond dimension is $13$ for the model of Eq.~\eqref{eq:annni_H}. As noted in Section~\ref{sec:transverse:network}, the virtual bond dimension of the time evolution \ac{MPO} becomes the local physical dimension of the temporal chain after moving to the transverse picture. Consequently, \textit{VD2} carries a larger local dimension than cheaper constructions like the ones of Zaletel et al.~\cite{zaletel2015}, which are only first order in a single application. For a given accuracy, the latter thus demand a smaller time step, while \textit{VD2} can hold the same accuracy at a bigger $\delta t$. The larger step shortens the temporal chain at fixed physical time and lowers the number of truncation steps, which is what makes the long-time calculations of Section~\ref{sec:results} affordable. Appendix~\ref{app:mpo} gives the explicit tensor and a measurement of the convergence order of each operator.

The temporal transfer matrix is assembled from a single tensor of the time evolution \ac{MPO}, repeated along the chain, so the tensor we extract must be one that a site in the bulk would carry, with every interaction present. We therefore exponentiate the Hamiltonian on a chain long enough for its central site to have all of its partners inside it, five sites for an \ac{NNN} model, and take the tensor at the centre. Every result in this thesis uses this construction.
